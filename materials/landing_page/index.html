The Geography

Why is Namibia
so dry?

The cold Benguela Current chills the coastal air, suppressing rainfall. Namibia receives only about 285 mm of rain a year, the driest country south of the Sahara.

Of all the rain that falls, only about 2% becomes river runoff. Roughly 83% evaporates straight back into the air.

SOURCE: Atlas of Namibia · British Geological Survey
The Crisis · 2024

A drought that reached
every region

Namibia's worst drought in a century struck all 14 regions at once, an unprecedented nationwide crisis. Around 1.2 million people (40% of those assessed) faced acute food insecurity.

To relieve pressure on dwindling pasture and water, the government authorised culling 723 wild animals, including 83 elephants. Water rationing was introduced in Windhoek.

SOURCE: IPC (2024) · Namibia MEFT · UN OCHA
Your Role

What can you do?

Small actions create measurable impact:

  • Turn off the tap while brushing teeth saves ~12 L
  • Fix a dripping tap at home 20 L/day
  • Take a 2-minute shorter shower saves 18 L
  • Run appliances only when full ~12 L/wash
  • Fix a leaking toilet 200 L/day
